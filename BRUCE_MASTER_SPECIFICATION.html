1. The BRUCE Simulation Model & Working Principles

BRUCE is an agile bipedal humanoid robot standing 0.45m at nominal pelvis height with a total mass of 4.2 kg. The simulation environment accurately models the physical robot's mechanical structure, actuator dynamics, and environmental contact mechanics:

  • Mechanical Topology (16 Actuated DOF): 10 proprietary BEAR planetary actuators (5 per leg: hip yaw, hip roll, hip pitch, knee pitch, ankle pitch) providing high torque density and low backdrive friction (clamped to ±160 Nm in simulation). Upper body features 6 Dynamixel servos (3 per arm: shoulder pitch, shoulder roll, elbow pitch) providing compliant arm swing and manipulation stances (clamped to ±35 Nm).
  • MuJoCo Contact Dynamics (1000 Hz): The physics loop steps at 1000 Hz (dt=0.001s) using projected Gauss-Seidel optimization with elliptic friction cones and soft-contact parameters (solref=0.004, solimp=0.9). This eliminates foot contact chattering and penetration spikes during single-stance and double-stance transitions.
  • 25×25m Hybrid Virtual Arena: Combines an indoor robotics lab (corridors, partition rooms, 1.0m doorways, 4 landmark columns for visual SLAM loop closure) with an outdoor obstacle playground (3-step staircase, 12° incline ramp, regulation soccer goal, pushable crates, barrels, and delivery table).

2. Architectural Decisions & Design Rationale ("The Why")

Every major design choice was made to resolve specific physical, mathematical, or performance trade-offs:

  • Decision 1: MuJoCo over Gazebo Classic: Gazebo's ODE engine produced foot vibration, penetration impulses, high latency, and low frame rates (<25 FPS) for bipedal walking. MuJoCo solves contacts via convex optimization, yielding rock-solid stability at 1000 Hz and native 60+ FPS GLFW graphics with hardware mouse locking.
  • Decision 2: Custom GLFW Pipeline over Passive Viewer: MuJoCo's built-in passive viewer lacks continuous key-hold tracking and cannot lock the mouse cursor for 360° camera orbit. A custom GLFW rendering loop with MjvScene, MjrContext, and mjr_overlay was built for responsive game controls and live HUD displays.
  • Decision 3: Camera-Relative Locomotion Math: Projecting WASD vectors against camera azimuth angle θ ensures that pressing W always moves forward in the direction the camera is looking, matching modern third-person action games.
  • Decision 4: Walking Stabilization (Counter-Pitch Torque & Knee Lift): Early forward walking stalled due to foot toe-stubbing (8mm clearance dragged on the floor) and pelvis thrust tipping moments (+18 Nm tilting the torso forward >20°). Increasing knee lift to >35mm with ankle dorsiflexion and adding dynamic counter-pitch torque (τ = -Fpropel · 0.22 Nm) stabilized torso pitch within 3°–6° with zero backward drift.
  • Decision 5: Two-Process Decoupled IPC Architecture: Off-screen RGB-D rendering and 1,152-ray LiDAR sweeps take 15–30 ms per frame. Executing them inside the game controller would drop frame rates below 20 FPS and destabilize the balance loop. Decoupling into two processes via a 1,032-byte POSIX shared memory buffer ensures 60+ FPS gameplay with zero perception latency.
  • Decision 6: Spherical 3D LiDAR with Body Exclusion: Raycasting 1,152 beams (16 elevation rings × 72 azimuths) using mujoco.mj_ray() with bodyexclude=self.base_body_id ensures rays pass through BRUCE's own chest and head meshes, preventing false self-occlusion.
  • Decision 7: Upper-Body Biomechanics & Aesthetics: Eliminated the unnatural horizontal T-pose by rolling resting shoulders to ±1.38 rad and flexing elbows to ±0.50 rad (~28° forward bend). Added phase-coupled arm swing opposite to legs, 0.45 Hz breathing micro-sway, and contoured head, visor, and chest armor geoms.
  • Decision 8: Dual Mouse Mode (Game Look vs Object Dragging): Key M toggles between Game Mode (cursor locked to camera orbit) and Manipulation Mode (cursor unlocked). In Manipulation Mode, raycasting calculates world floor points and applies spring-damper forces (F = 80Δp - 12v) to drag arena objects live.
  • Decision 9: Inline A* Planner vs External Nav2 Loop: External Nav2 costmaps over ROS introduce 50–200 ms latency. An inline A* planner operating on a 200×200 LiDAR occupancy grid inside the controller avoids obstacles at 60 FPS without feedback latency, while streaming costmaps to ROS 2 for visualization.
  • Decision 10: Snap Environment Isolation: Purging Snap-inherited variables (GTK_PATH) in all bash launchers prevents Qt5 in RViz2 from loading mismatched glibc 2.31 libraries from Snap, guaranteeing 100% reliable startup on Ubuntu 24.04.

3. Robot Functions, Locomotion States & Feature Catalog

BRUCE features a hierarchical state machine handling dynamic locomotion, physical interaction, and autonomous tasks:

Figure 2: Hierarchical Posture & Locomotion State Machine Transitions
  • STAND (Idle Balance): Maintains pelvis height at z=0.44m with active roll/pitch PD attitude dampers and organic 0.45 Hz breathing micro-sway.
  • WALK & SPRINT (Omnidirectional): Stepping frequency scales from 1.25 Hz (0.24 m/s) to 1.50 Hz (0.48 m/s). Lateral hip roll weight-transfer sway (0.065 rad) stabilizes single-stance phases, while arms swing in opposition with dynamic elbow bending.
  • CROUCH & CROUCH-WALK: Flexes knees and hips to drop pelvis to z=0.31m. Arms adopt a forward guard stance. WASD allows low-profile stealth walking under overhead obstacles.
  • 4-PHASE JUMP: Executes pre-jump compression dip (0.0–0.15s), explosive thrust extension (+160N impulse, 0.15–0.30s), ballistic airborne flight (0.30–0.65s), and compliant touchdown landing absorption (0.65–1.00s).
  • DYNAMIC SOCCER KICK: 3-phase sequence: left stance leg bends for stability, right kicking leg pulls back, then snaps forward with dynamic forward propulsion to launch the ball with >50N force.
  • FORWARD PUSH STANCE: Drives forward with stiff, outstretched arms applying +35N continuous pushing force against target crates and obstacle blocks.
  • LIVE MOUSE DRAGGING: In Manipulation Mode (M key), clicking and dragging dynamic objects moves them across the floor in real time to create custom obstacles.
  • AUTONOMOUS CLICK-TO-NAVIGATE: Double-clicking the floor plants a glowing green Goal Marker. Pressing N activates autonomous A* path planning using inline LiDAR obstacle avoidance.
